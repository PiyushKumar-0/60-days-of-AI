The Real Decision

You're not choosing a job title, you're choosing where to point months of learning effort right now. "Software Developer" is broad and prestigious-sounding, but Front End and Full Stack are more concrete starting lanes with faster feedback loops.

The trade-off: breadth (Software Dev, unclear first step) vs. speed-to-employable (Front End, narrower but portfolio-ready fast) vs. balance (Full Stack, more to learn but more hireable range).

It's hard because this feels like a "dream job" identity choice, not just a skill-stack choice — so every option feels loaded with more meaning than it actually carries.

Premortem — 12 Months From Now, It Failed
Option A — Software Developer
  • You spent months exploring broad CS topics without a shippable project, so you have nothing to show recruiters.
  • The lack of a defined curriculum meant motivation faded without visible progress.
  • You applied for jobs but couldn't articulate a specific specialization, so you got filtered out early.

Early warning sign: 6 weeks in, you still can't describe what you're building.

Prevention: Pick one concrete project in week one and force every learning session to serve it.

Option B — Full Stack
  • You tried to learn front end and back end simultaneously and never got deep enough in either to be hireable.
  • You burned out juggling too many technologies at once.
  • Your portfolio projects looked generic because you rushed both halves.

Early warning sign: You're context-switching daily between frontend and backend tutorials with no finished project.

Prevention: Sequence it — front end first to working competence, then layer backend onto one real project.

7-Day Test Plan
Day 1-2Research: compare 3 real entry-level job postings each for "Software Developer" and "Full Stack Developer" in your target market — note actual required skills.
Day 3-4Experiment: build one tiny end-to-end feature (a simple UI hitting a simple API) to feel what full-stack work is actually like day-to-day.
Day 5-6Conversation: talk to one working developer (any track) about what their actual day looks like and what got them hired.
Day 7Decide: if the mini end-to-end build felt energizing rather than draining, commit to Full Stack; if you kept wanting to go deeper into logic/backend only, lean Software Dev.
The Verdict
Full Stack Developer

It wins on the matrix (52 vs 49) mainly on financial safety and reversibility — it's the most hireable entry point and doesn't actually block you from going deeper into "Software Developer" territory later.

Your gut said "Software," but nothing you've told me makes that a specific, checkable reason — it reads more like the more impressive-sounding label than a considered pick.

What could flip it: If the 7-day test shows you're consistently more energized by backend logic than by shipping visible UI, Software Dev becomes the stronger long-term bet.

Hard truth: "Software Developer" isn't a plan — it's what you call yourself after you've already built something specific enough to get hired for.
